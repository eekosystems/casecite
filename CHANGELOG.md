# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Removed
- **The authority map** (`/case-citations`, `POST /authority-map/analyze`, `GET`/`DELETE /authority-map/{run_id}`, the chat intent that answered "give me all the case law for this file" with a background audit, and the `authority_map` record on chat messages). It extracted the propositions of one uploaded document and looked up quote-verified CourtListener authority for each. The Matter Strategy brief already does that job, across the whole matter rather than one file: it derives the position, strengths and weaknesses from every scoped document and attaches to each point only authorities whose supporting quote verifies verbatim against the opinion text, within a time budget. Keeping two entry points to one capability confused evaluators, so the standalone feature goes. Those chat asks now route to the strategy brief (the classifier's retired `authority_map` label is read as `strategy`). The verbatim quote check every feature relies on lives in `services/quote_verify.py`. Migration 018 drops `authority_map_runs`, `authority_mappings` and `chat_messages.authority_map`; the `authority_map.use` permission is gone (file extraction and conversion need a document or contract permission); `AUTHORITY_MAP_MAX_DOCUMENT_WINDOWS` and `AUTHORITY_MAP_MAX_OPINION_WINDOWS` are no longer read. The opened-document view in Research keeps its inline anchors for the conversation's verified claims.

### Added
- **Workflows: approval gates, conditions, versions, rich outputs, run controls and notifications.** A new step kind, **approval**, pauses the run (`awaiting_approval`) and ends the job until an approver decides: `approvers` is the owner, any member of the workflow's matter, or named members (checked on save); `POST /workflows/{id}/runs/{run_id}/approve` continues the run from the next step with every earlier output reloaded, `/reject` ends it as `rejected` with the later steps skipped; the decision, who made it, their note and the time are kept on the step. Every step may carry a **condition** (`{step, path, op, value}` over an earlier output, with `exists`, `not_exists`, `equals`, `not_equals`, `contains`, `gt`, `lt`, `gte`, `lte`, `count_gt`, `count_eq`): a false rule records the step as `skipped_condition` with what was found, and the run goes on. **Validation while editing**: `POST /workflows/validate` reports every problem with the step and field it concerns (unknown kinds, bad parameters, duplicate names, references to later or missing steps in inputs, conditions, `from_steps`, `include_outputs_from` and `{{templates}}`, a compare step with a fixed baseline but no document, empty drafting instructions), and the editor shows them under each step and disables Save until clean. **Versions**: every change to the steps writes a `workflow_versions` row and bumps `version`; a run records `workflow_version`; `GET /workflows/{id}/versions`, `GET .../versions/{n}` and `POST .../versions/{n}/restore` (a restore is a new version; history is never rewritten); `POST /workflows/{id}/duplicate` copies a workflow the caller can read into one they own; `POST .../archive` and `/unarchive` hide a workflow from the default list (`?include_archived=true`) and refuse runs. **Run controls**: `POST .../runs/{run_id}/steps/{index}/retry` re-runs one step and everything after it, `/skip` passes over a failed step; both owner-or-runner; a run opens by link (`/workflows/{id}?run={run_id}`) and can be compared step by step with the previous run; `GET /workflows/runs` lists the newest runs across every workflow the caller can read, optionally one matter's. **Outputs** are rendered per kind (the extracted rows with a link to the review table, per-document issues with severity and quotes, clause differences side by side, the draft with Word download, the research answer with sources, citation treatment, the approval decision), analysis outputs now carry the issues themselves (capped per document), and every stored output is cut at `WORKFLOW_MAX_OUTPUT_BYTES` (default 512 KB) with the cut fields listed. The editor picks fixed inputs, baselines and references from documents by name instead of typed ids, and shows names in summaries and run cards; `compare_contracts` gains `baseline` (`first_input` or `document`). **Notifications**: with `WORKFLOW_NOTIFICATIONS=true` and a SendGrid key, the runner (and the approvers, when a run waits) is emailed when a run completes, fails, waits for approval or is rejected, with the workflow, run time, failed step and a link, never document text; each user can opt out in Settings (`workflow_email_notifications`); a delivery failure is logged and never touches the run. The list gains search, filters (mine, shared, needs approval, last run failed, archived) and a strip of recent runs. Audited as `workflow_run_awaiting_approval`, `workflow_run_approved`, `workflow_run_rejected`, `workflow_step_retried`, `workflow_step_skipped`, `workflow_duplicated`, `workflow_archived`, `workflow_unarchived` and `workflow_version_restored`, ids and counts only. Migration 020. See `docs/workflows.md`.
- **Tabular review: a review layer, templates, run history and richer exports.** Every cell now carries a human layer on top of the model's answer: a reviewer may **override** the value (the model's value is kept; the effective value is the override), **approve** or **flag** it, and leave a **note**; who reviewed and when is recorded, and the cell keeps a bounded **history** of the model's answers, overrides, approvals, flags and clears. A re-run never undoes that work: the model's new value is written, the override and note stay, and an approval drops back to pending only when the model's value changed and the cell is not overridden; the grid's *Changed* filter shows cells whose last two model answers differ. The grid gains status chips with counts (needs review, unverified, not found, errors, flagged, approved, changed), a text filter under every column, typed sorting (dates, money, numbers and durations sort as quantities, blanks last), row and column selection with bulk approve / flag / reset / re-run, approve-column and re-run-column on each header, sticky header and document column, resizable columns remembered per table, row windowing for tables of hundreds of documents, and keyboard navigation (arrows, Enter opens the source, E edits, A approves, F flags). Clicking a cell opens a **source panel** showing the document's indexed text with the quote highlighted and scrolled into view (an unverified quote is located by search and marked approximate), beside the model value, the override editor, approve / flag, the note and the history. A **summary strip** shows review progress and per-column outcomes, and the run's progress while it runs. **Schema templates**: four built-in field sets (NDA, master services agreement, commercial lease, employment agreement; 8–12 described fields each) plus the firm's saved schemas, personal or shared with a matter; the create form starts from any of them, the schema editor loads a template, reorders fields and saves the current fields as a template. Documents can be **added after creation** (new rows extracted straight away) and removed per row. Every run is recorded in a **run history** with its scope and counts, including how many cells changed. **Exports** gain `format=json`, a column subset (`fields`), approved-only (`reviewed_only`), a status filter (`status`) and `include_quotes=false`; the workbook gains a *Review* sheet (model value, override, effective value, status, reviewer, note) and a *Summary* sheet (per-column counts), and Results shows effective values with overrides in a distinct style. See `docs/tabular-review.md`.
- Tabular review API additions: `PATCH /review-tables/{id}/cells/{document_id}/{field_key}` and `POST /review-tables/{id}/cells/bulk` (owner and matter members review alike); `GET/POST /review-tables/schemas` and `PATCH/DELETE /review-tables/schemas/{id}` (built-ins are read-only, ids `builtin:<key>`); `POST /review-tables/{id}/documents` (with `run: true` extracts only the new rows) and `DELETE /review-tables/{id}/documents/{document_id}`; `GET /review-tables/{id}/documents/{document_id}/text` (the text the offsets refer to); `GET /review-tables/{id}/runs`. Table summaries carry `approved_count` and `flagged_count`; a started run returns its `run_id`. Audited as `review_cell_reviewed`, `review_cells_bulk_reviewed`, `review_schema_created/updated/deleted`, `review_table_documents_added`, `review_table_document_removed`, with counts only. Migration 019 adds the review columns to `review_cells` and the `review_schemas` and `review_runs` tables.
- **Word add-in** (`word-addin/`). A task-pane add-in for Microsoft Word that works against the firm's own CaseCite server: *Analyze this document* runs the contract review on the open document and lists issues and key terms in the pane, clicking one selects the quoted passage in Word (by text search, falling back to the first sentence and then a shorter opening when Word's 255-character search limit or formatting gets in the way); *Insert as tracked change* replaces that passage with the issue's suggested language with Track Changes on, and restores the document's own tracking setting afterwards; *Research* asks a question over the firm's documents (or one matter's) and inserts the answer at the cursor with one footnote per source (a "Sources" list on hosts without WordApi 1.5); *Settings* holds the server address, the matter and sign-out. Every AI-generated insertion ends with the same verify-before-relying notice the exports carry. The pane is a client only; documents go to the CaseCite server and from there to the configured provider, as from the browser. Vite + React + TypeScript, Office.js from Microsoft's CDN, 33 unit tests with the Office globals stubbed; `scripts/make-manifest.mjs` writes the manifest for a deployed origin, and `word-addin/README.md` covers sideloading on Windows, Mac and the web, Centralized Deployment, and what is not supported. The server gains `WORD_ADDIN_ORIGIN`, which adds exactly that origin to the CORS allow list (nothing changes when unset).
- **Device-code sign-in** for clients that cannot hold a cookie session (the add-in's task pane runs in a sandboxed browser). `POST /auth/device/start` (unauthenticated, rate-limited) returns a secret device code and an eight-character user code from an alphabet without look-alike characters, shown as `XXXX-XXXX`; the signed-in web user approves or denies that code on the new `/device` page (`POST /auth/device/approve` and `/deny`, cookie-authenticated and CSRF-protected, 404 for an unknown or expired code, 409 for one already decided, refused for an account that `REQUIRE_MFA` obliges to enrol); the device polls `POST /auth/device/token` and receives `authorization_pending`, `slow_down` (polling faster than `interval`), `expired_token`, `access_denied` or, once, a session issued through the same path as a login with both tokens in the body. Pending codes live in Redis (in-memory fallback) under a hash of the device code for `DEVICE_CODE_TTL` seconds (default 600) and are single-use. The session is labelled **Word add-in** in `GET /auth/sessions` (every session now carries `client`, "web" for the rest) and can be ended there. A device session's refresh token carries a `device` client claim: `/auth/refresh` accepts a `refresh_token` in the JSON body only for such a token, rotates it and returns the new one in the body; cookie sessions are unchanged. The CSRF middleware no longer validates a state-changing request that carries no auth cookie at all (a bearer-only request has no ambient credential a cross-site page could ride on, and a custom header forces a CORS preflight); any auth cookie keeps validation on exactly as before. Audited as `auth.device.approved` / `auth.device.denied`, and the issued session as a login with `method: device`.
- **Workflows.** `/workflows` saves a repeatable legal process as an ordered, linear list of typed steps and runs it over any set of documents as one background job. Six step kinds, each a thin adapter over an existing service and never code: *extract fields* (a tabular review of the step's documents against an inline field schema; the run creates a review table the runner owns, named for the workflow and step and tagged with the run), *analyse contracts* (the full contract pipeline on each document, bounded by `WORKFLOW_MAX_DOCUMENTS_PER_STEP`, default 25; a document that cannot be read or analysed is recorded, not fatal, unless every document fails), *compare contracts* (clause by clause against a baseline, the first input by default, up to three others), *draft a document* (instructions with `{{step.field}}` substitution from earlier outputs, the user's practice profile and playbook attached as the drafting routes attach them, earlier outputs and up to four reference documents as reference text, the case-law guard as everywhere), *deep research* (the question templated from earlier outputs, planned and run as a deep-research run over the step's documents, with the planner's tools optionally restricted) and *check citations* (reporter and Westlaw citations given explicitly or found in earlier outputs, through the treatment scan, capped by `WORKFLOW_MAX_CITATIONS_PER_STEP`, default 10). Each step declares where its documents come from: the run's inputs, ids fixed in the workflow, or the documents an earlier step read. Steps execute in order; each step's status, output, documents read and work product created are written as they happen, so a run that stops can be read in full; a failure stops the run and marks the rest skipped; **resume** re-submits from the first unfinished step with every earlier output reloaded, running the steps the run started with even if the workflow has been edited since. Cancelling the job marks the run cancelled, and it can be resumed too. Substitution is a plain path walk over earlier outputs (dictionary keys and list indexes, nothing evaluated); an unknown path renders empty and is reported on the step. Validation on save: unknown kinds, bad parameters, duplicate names, and any reference to a later or nonexistent step are refused (422); a workflow holds at most `WORKFLOW_MAX_STEPS` (12). Three built-in templates (NDA intake, lease review, due-diligence pass) instantiate into a workflow of the caller's own. The owner edits and deletes; members of the workflow's matter read and run it, with their own key and their own document access; anyone else gets 404. "Run on matter" uses every document filed under the workflow's matter. See `docs/workflows.md`.
- Workflows API: `GET/POST /workflows`, `GET /workflows/templates`, `POST /workflows/from-template/{key}`, `GET/PATCH/DELETE /workflows/{id}`, `POST /workflows/{id}/run` (202 + a job to poll; with no ids and a matter-filed workflow, the matter's documents), `GET /workflows/{id}/runs`, `GET /workflows/{id}/runs/{run_id}` (with every step's status and output) and `POST /workflows/{id}/runs/{run_id}/resume`. Every input document, fixed document, baseline and reference must be readable by the runner at that moment. Runs are never retried by the job queue. Audited as `workflow_created`, `workflow_updated`, `workflow_deleted`, `workflow_run_started`, `workflow_run_resumed`, `workflow_run_completed` and `workflow_run_failed` with ids, counts and step kinds only. New tables `workflows`, `workflow_runs` and `workflow_step_runs` (migration 017). Gated by `contracts.use`. The sidebar's Analysis Mode list gains Workflows (Ctrl+Shift+5).
- **Deep research.** A "Deep research" mode in the Research view for questions that need more than one retrieval. A planner proposes 3–8 sub-questions, each tagged with the sources it may use (the firm's indexed documents, CourtListener case law, or both); the user edits the plan — rewrite, remove, add or re-target a sub-question — and nothing is searched until they run it. The run is one background job: sub-questions are researched concurrently (`DEEP_RESEARCH_CONCURRENCY`, default 3), every passage a tool returns is given a server-assigned reference, one bounded call per sub-question writes claims that cite those references and copy a supporting quote, and a sub-question may propose one follow-up search. Every quote is located in the passage it cites; one that cannot be found is kept and marked unverified, never dropped, and a reference that names no real passage is removed and counted. One synthesis call writes the final answer over the sub-answers and the cited passages; its references are joined back in code, model prose naming a case that appears in no passage is redacted by the existing case-law guard, and the reporter citations of the authorities the answer cites go through the citation check (the treatment-language scan, not a citator). For case law the cited passage is cut from the opinion's real text (the search snippet is used, and labelled, only when the opinion cannot be fetched). The result carries the answer, key findings and open questions, a verification line (quotes verified, unverified, references stripped, case names redacted, authorities checked), the sources with per-passage verified-quote counts and treatment, a per-sub-question step log (every search and its query, passages found or the error, the follow-up, the claims and their quotes), and a scope note that says what was searched and that documents were not read in full. Runs are bounded by `DEEP_RESEARCH_MAX_SUBQUESTIONS` (8), `DEEP_RESEARCH_MAX_TOOL_CALLS` (24), `DEEP_RESEARCH_MAX_PASSAGES` (60) and `DEEP_RESEARCH_FOLLOWUP_DEPTH` (1). The document scope follows research chat: an explicit file pick, else the active matter's documents, else everything the caller can read. Past runs reopen from the panel's history, scoped to the active matter. See `docs/deep-research.md`.
- Deep research API: `POST /deep-research/plan` (synchronous; the plan, the scope it would search and the server's caps), `POST /deep-research/run` (202 + a job to poll; persists the run), `GET /deep-research` (optionally `?matter_id=`), `GET /deep-research/{id}` and `DELETE /deep-research/{id}`. Owner or matter member reads; owner deletes; anyone else gets 404. Runs are not retried by the job queue; a failed or cancelled run is recorded as such. Audited as `deep_research_started`, `deep_research_completed` and `deep_research_deleted` with ids and counts only. New table `deep_research_runs` (migration 016). Gated by `chat.use`.
- **Tabular review.** `/review-tables` reviews many documents against one user-defined schema: name the fields you want (text, date, money, number, yes/no or duration, each with an optional hint), pick the documents (every indexed document you can read, or the active matter's), and run. The run is one background job that extracts the documents concurrently (`REVIEW_TABLE_CONCURRENCY`, default 4) and writes each document's row as it finishes, so a partially complete sweep is already visible; the job reports progress per document. Every value carries the verbatim quote that supports it and its offsets in the document text, with the same quote-and-verify contract as the contract key-terms table: a quote that is found character for character marks the cell *verified*, one that is not leaves the value *unverified* and de-emphasised, and a field the excerpts do not establish is *not found*. A document that has been through contract analysis contributes its tagged clause spans to the excerpts; otherwise the preamble and the passages most relevant to the schema are sent, within the same budget. The grid re-runs a cell, a row or a column, shows the cost estimate (documents × fields, approximate input tokens, model) before a run, and exports an `.xlsx` whose Results sheet links every answered cell to its row on a Citations sheet (quote, offsets, verified, status), or a flat CSV. The owner edits the schema and scope and deletes; members of the table's matter read, run and export; anyone else gets 404.
- Tabular review API: `GET/POST /review-tables`, `GET/PATCH/DELETE /review-tables/{id}`, `GET /review-tables/{id}/estimate`, `POST /review-tables/{id}/run` (202 + a job to poll; optional `document_ids` / `field_keys` narrow it to a re-run) and `GET /review-tables/{id}/export?format=xlsx|csv`. Documents put in scope must be readable by the caller at that moment (owner or matter member), so a colleague's private document cannot be reviewed through a shared table; one that later becomes unreadable is reported as an error cell, not dropped. Runs, completions and exports are audited with ids and counts only. New tables `review_tables` and `review_cells` (migration 015). Gated by the `contracts.use` permission.
- **Matter workspace.** `/matters` lists the matters you belong to and creates new ones; `/matters/:id` is one matter's workspace with tabs for the documents, research chats and contract analyses filed under it, its members, and an activity feed drawn from the audit trail's database copy (entries about the matter and about the documents and analyses currently filed under it). A matter selector in the header sets an *active matter*; Research narrows retrieval to that matter's documents unless a file or folder is picked explicitly, and the Contracts and Drafting document pickers show only that matter's documents. "All matters" (the default) leaves every view as before. The sidebar's Documents section gains a Matters entry.
- Matter API additions: `PATCH /matters/{id}` (rename, client name; owner only), `POST /matters/{id}/transfer` (hand the matter to an existing member, who becomes owner while the caller stays a member; owner only; audited as `matter_ownership_transferred`), `GET /matters/{id}/documents`, `GET /matters/{id}/chats`, `GET /matters/{id}/analyses` and `GET /matters/{id}/activity` (members only; 404 for anyone else), and `POST /matters/{id}/documents/move` to file documents under a matter in bulk. A move is allowed for the caller's own documents and for documents filed under a matter the caller owns (the same rule as deleting them); any other document answers 404 and nothing moves. The vector chunks are re-tagged before the registry changes so the old matter's members drop out of the search scope, and a chunk that cannot be re-tagged leaves its document where it was (503). Moving into the personal matter returns a document to owner-only visibility. Chat session summaries now carry `user_id` and `matter_id`.

### Changed
- **License: Apache License 2.0**, replacing the Elastic License 2.0 from this version on (1.3.0 and earlier remain under ELv2). CaseCite is now OSI open source with an explicit patent grant; hosting it for others no longer needs a separate agreement. `NOTICE.md` carries the project copyright line Apache expects, and the NOTICE generator's compatibility check additionally refuses BUSL, Commons Clause and non-commercial terms. The Settings dialog footer links to the source repository.
- README: screenshots of Research, Knowledge Base, Contracts and Citation Check, captured from the real UI against mocked data by `frontend/e2e/screenshots.spec.ts` (run with `SCREENSHOTS=1`).
- **iManage and NetDocuments connectors are experimental and off by default.** Both are implemented against the vendors' published APIs but have not been tested against a live tenant, so the shipped feature list no longer claims them: they are not registered unless `EXPERIMENTAL_CONNECTORS=true`, which means they are absent from `GET /connectors` and every route for them is refused (400, the same answer an unknown provider gets), and the startup log says they are disabled as experimental. With the flag on they behave as before and their entries in `GET /connectors` (and `GET /connectors/{type}/status`) carry `"experimental": true`; other connectors carry `false`. The code is unchanged otherwise. Anyone who tests them against a real tenant is welcome to report the result.

### Internal
- Routers raise domain exceptions (`app/exceptions.py`: `NotFoundError`, `ForbiddenError`, ...) handled by one handler that produces the same responses as before, instead of 181 hand-written `HTTPException`s.
- The chat endpoint and the largest service functions (RAG answer generation, judge metrics, contract analysis, judge-intel building, citation validation, clause analysis) are split into single-purpose helpers; no function in those files exceeds about 80 lines. Behaviour, logging and audit calls are unchanged.
- mypy is blocking in CI through `scripts/mypy_ratchet.py`: each package has a ceiling in `backend/mypy-baseline.json`, clean packages stay clean, and the baseline only goes down. `middleware`, `utils` (except SQLAlchemy `Column` typing) and the authority mapper are at zero.
- Frontend: the `--gold-*` theme variables, which had been greys driven by the branding primary colour, are `--primary-*`; the CSS-module object components received through a prop named `s` is `styles`.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-10-28

### Security
- Connector OAuth callbacks are bound to the browser that started the flow with a signed HttpOnly cookie; a callback arriving without it, or with another user's binding, is refused (403) and audited.
- Saving admin connector credentials merges into the stored values, so a field omitted from the request keeps its value instead of being blanked; an empty string clears just that field.
- Invitations write their audit entry before the account is committed, so with `AUDIT_FAIL_CLOSED=true` a failed audit write refuses the invite rather than creating an account whose temporary password is lost.
- A failed permission-override load serves the defaults for one short back-off window without caching them and logs at error level.
- Azure AD SSO normalises the email in the identity service and the login route, and provisions a fresh local id instead of reusing the Azure object id, so a deleted user who signs in again is not caught by the deletion tombstone.
- Authority maps require `document_id` to name a document the caller owns or can read through a matter (404 otherwise).
- Dropbox disconnect mints a fresh access token from the refresh token before revoking; revocation failures never block user deletion.
- The nginx site config inside the image is root-owned 0644 instead of writable by the application user.
- An access token whose user row no longer exists (deleted by hand, or a database restored from before the account existed) is rejected outside DEBUG instead of staying valid for its remaining lifetime.
- `AUDIT_HMAC_KEY` must be at least 32 characters; the audit chain uses its first 32 bytes, so a short value silently weakened the tamper evidence.
- A second process serving the same data directory (`uvicorn --workers 2`, gunicorn, a second container replica) is refused at startup through an advisory lock on `data/.instance.lock`, not only when `UVICORN_WORKERS` says so. Linux only; skipped on Windows.
- Chat error logs outside DEBUG record the exception type only, so a parse error can no longer quote privileged chat text into long-retained logs.
- Mako 1.3.x advisory (CVE-2026-102991) resolved by bumping the lock.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-10-04

### Added
- Every generated artifact carries a review notice — "AI-generated. Verify every statement, citation and clause against the source documents and current law before relying on it." — in the UI (research answers, contract analyses, redlines, drafts, judge briefs, citation checks, authority maps, contract/case/tool chats) and as a closing paragraph in every Word, Markdown and PDF export. The drafting prompts that told the model to omit caveats and never suggest consulting an attorney are gone.
- Retrieved documents, CourtListener opinions and contract text are passed to the model inside explicit untrusted-content delimiters with a standing rule that instructions found inside them are data to be ignored (and mentioned); the user-query sanitizer no longer rewrites legitimate legal phrasing such as "act as attorney-in-fact".
- `NOTICE.md` lists every bundled third-party license (116 Python, 44 npm packages; none GPL/AGPL/SSPL). Contributions are accepted under the Developer Certificate of Origin (`git commit -s`), documented in CONTRIBUTING.md and the PR template.
- `API_TIMEOUT` (default 180 s) is now applied to every LLM and embedding client, with two SDK retries; previously the SDK's 600 s default was used and the setting was never read.
- Long-document drafting: set a target length in the Drafting workspace and the draft is planned first (sections, briefs, what each section must leave to others, shared definitions, style guide) from the full reference documents; after review, every section is drafted in parallel in its own context window with the full references and the whole plan, then a reconcile pass fixes cross-references, numbering and defined-term drift. `POST /contract-analysis/draft/plan` and `/draft/generate`.
- Revisions of long drafts are scoped: only the sections an instruction touches are rewritten (a selected clause routes straight to its section), the rest is left byte-for-byte.
- Background jobs report progress (`GET /jobs/{id}` → `progress: {message, fraction}`); the Drafting workspace shows it while sections are drafted.
- `LLM_CONTEXT_WINDOW` to declare the chat model's context size for custom/local endpoints whose names don't say.
- Password flows in the UI: a `/reset-password` page for the emailed link, a Change Password dialog under Settings → Security, and a forced password change at first sign-in for invited users. On a production install the first (admin) account is registered with `REGISTRATION_BOOTSTRAP_TOKEN`; the sign-up form has a "Setup token" field for it.
- Admin UI: deactivate/reactivate, delete, export, force sign-out and two-factor reset in the Users tab; an Audit Log tab with filters, an integrity check and JSONL export (`GET /admin/audit/export`); active sessions and "Sign out everywhere" under Security; a Remove button for stored API keys.
- `REQUIRE_MFA` makes two-factor enrolment mandatory; `AUDIT_FAIL_CLOSED` fails a request whose audit entry cannot be written.
- `EMBEDDING_BASE_URL` sends embeddings to an OpenAI-compatible server you run, so with `OPENAI_BASE_URL` set as well no document text leaves your network.
- AI provider allowlist (`APPROVED_AI_PROVIDERS`), enforced on every LLM and embedding call against the endpoint that carries the request.
- Scoped chat: questions restricted to selected files or a folder search only those documents, give every selected file a share of the passages, and report the measured similarity.
- Connector sync is scoped to a folder or an explicit whole-account confirmation, with the same size cap, type checks and audit events as picker imports. Filevine is admin-only and enabled with `FILEVINE_ENABLED=true`.

### Fixed
- Drafting no longer silently truncates reference documents (and the draft being revised) at 40,000 characters — about ten pages; the budget is now derived from the model's context window.
- Redline .docx export renders from the text extracted from the stored original file instead of the vector-chunk reconstruction (which repeated text at every chunk seam); stored edits are re-anchored by their verbatim original text, unlocatable ones are listed in the document, multi-paragraph deletions are tracked per paragraph, and a character-level fidelity check refuses to produce a document whose plain + deleted text differs from the source.
- Chunk-seam de-duplication: consecutive vector chunks share an overlap run; contract analysis no longer sees (or quotes, or redlines) that run twice.
- Citation check ("Shepardize") no longer reports a case as good law after finding negative treatment language. The verdict is tri-state (`is_good_law: true | null`, never `false`), the most-cited citing opinions are scanned alongside the most recent, bare "rejected"/"erroneous" no longer count as negative, "overruled on other grounds" is a caution, and the not-a-citator caveat is shown with the badge. The tool is now labelled "Citation Check".
- Settings save failures are surfaced (toast + rollback) instead of closing the modal as if saved; wrong-password and other credential-endpoint 401s show the server's message instead of triggering a token refresh.
- The sidebar and Sources panel are reachable again on tablets and narrow windows: the responsive stylesheet is now wired to header toggles (with backdrop, Escape, and route-change close) instead of hiding both drawers with no way back.
- Contract review no longer rewrites span-less risk findings ("uncapped liability") as "Addressed" — only absence-type findings are put to the model for absence verification; risks keep their severity and are labelled unverified.
- Long-draft reconcile is checked before it is accepted: a `finish_reason` of `length` on any section or the reconcile pass is treated as a failure, every planned section heading must survive, and each section must stay within ±15% of its drafted length; otherwise the assembled sections are kept and the note names the section that failed.
- Dev/test SQLite no longer shares one connection across every session (`StaticPool`): a session's rollback could discard another session's uncommitted write, which is what made seed-then-request tests flaky.
- Operator runbooks work against the shipped stack: `scripts/backup_database.sh` runs on the host (the image never had `pg_dump` or the script), dumps Postgres through the bundled container and archives the `/app/data` volume in the same run; `scripts/restore_database.sh` decrypts `.enc` backups, quotes identifiers, restores the volume with `--data`, and stops/starts the backend around the restore. `docs/secret-rotation.md` is rewritten for the real stack (`casecite` role, derived `DATABASE_URL`/`REDIS_URL`, `up -d` instead of `restart`, a warning that rotating `SECRET_KEY` makes stored documents unreadable), and `--re-encrypt` is now real: `python -m app.utils.reencrypt` rewrites the BYOK key store, connector credentials, TOTP secrets and instance secrets under a rotated `ENCRYPTION_SALT`.
- `docker-compose.prod.yml` runs `CASECITE_IMAGE:CASECITE_TAG` (default: the GHCR image CI publishes) and the frontend is copied out of that same image, so `pull` + `up -d` deploys and `CASECITE_TAG=<sha> … up -d` rolls back; the VPS deploy example pins each deploy to the commit SHA and no longer forwards `GITHUB_TOKEN` into a remote shell. The bundled Redis healthcheck authenticates (an unauthenticated `ping` returned NOAUTH and passed); `git` is no longer installed in the runtime image.

### Security
- Deleting a user now also removes their connector OAuth credential files (revoking Google and Dropbox tokens best-effort first) and their per-user settings file; previously a departed employee's Google Drive refresh token stayed on disk and usable.
- The app refuses to start with more than one uvicorn worker regardless of vector backend — the session store, document registry and connector caches are process-local and two workers silently corrupt them. `docs/deployment.md` says so plainly; Pinecone alone does not lift it.
- `TRUSTED_PROXIES` is required in the root `docker-compose.yml` (the Coolify/Traefik variant); defaulting it collapsed every client to the proxy's IP, so one user's failed logins locked out the whole firm.
- Bespoke Serif (a Fontshare font whose license forbids redistribution) is replaced by Source Serif 4 (OFL), bundled with the app.
- Validation errors no longer log the request body; submitted passwords and BYOK API keys stayed out of the container log stream only by luck before.
- `start.sh` no longer `chmod -R 755`s the data directory on every boot, which re-opened every 0600 secret file (instance secrets, BYOK key store, sessions, revoked tokens, audit logs, connector credentials).
- Audit entries are HMAC-signed over their full stored form, startup recomputes every recent signature instead of only checking linkage, and `AUDIT_HMAC_KEY_PREVIOUS` keeps a rotated key valid until the entries signed under it age out.
- Azure AD SSO mints tokens from the local account's roles rather than a hardcoded `attorney`; first-time SSO users get `AZURE_SSO_DEFAULT_ROLE` (viewer) or are refused when `AZURE_SSO_AUTO_PROVISION=false`; accounts are matched on the Azure object id, and an unused non-admin invite is adopted by email.
- Logout resets every in-memory store (research, contracts, drafts, judge intel, documents, keys), and the login password is cleared from state after sign-in.
- Sessions end 8 hours after sign-in (`SESSION_ABSOLUTE_TIMEOUT_HOURS`) and after 2 hours idle (`SESSION_IDLE_TIMEOUT_MINUTES`), with at most 5 per account. "Log out everywhere" and the admin force sign-out invalidate every access and refresh token of the account; replay of a rotated refresh token ends all of the account's sessions.
- MFA: codes are single-use, repeated failures lock the account's MFA endpoints, and disabling MFA requires the password as well as a code.
- The audit log is an HMAC-SHA256 chain in which every entry is linked to the one stored before it; startup verifies the most recent entries, and compressed archives stay queryable until the retention horizon.
- Uploads are bounded: decompression size and member limits for zip-based formats, page caps for PDFs and OCR.
- The page makes no third-party requests on load: fonts are bundled and the Google script is fetched only when the Google picker is opened. One Content-Security-Policy is shared by the backend, `Caddyfile` and `nginx.unified.conf`.
- Backups are encrypted by default (`BACKUP_ENCRYPTION_KEY`), checksummed, and verified in full before a restore drops anything. The bundled Redis runs `noeviction`.
- CI pins third-party actions to commit SHAs, audits the hash-pinned lock file, and runs `npm audit` and ESLint on every PR.
